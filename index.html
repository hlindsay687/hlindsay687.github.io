Conditions Treated

Comprehensive Neurological Care

Dr. Rubin treats a wide range of brain, spine, and nerve conditions, providing expert diagnosis and personalized treatment plans for each patient.

Brain Tumors

Evaluation and surgical treatment of benign and malignant brain tumors, including gliomas, meningiomas, and metastatic tumors.

Spine Disorders

Herniated discs, spinal stenosis, degenerative disc disease, spondylolisthesis, and other conditions causing back and neck pain.

Chronic Pain

Sciatica, radiculopathy, failed back surgery syndrome, and other chronic pain conditions affecting quality of life.

Nerve Compression

Carpal tunnel syndrome, ulnar nerve entrapment, and other peripheral nerve conditions causing numbness, tingling, or weakness.

Spinal Trauma

Vertebral fractures, spinal cord injuries, and trauma-related conditions requiring urgent or elective surgical intervention.

Vascular Conditions

Cerebral aneurysms, arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), and other vascular conditions affecting the brain and spine.

About Dr. Rubin

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Find answers to common questions about consultations, treatment, and what to expect from your care.

While some insurance plans require a referral from your primary care physician, many do not. We recommend checking with your insurance provider. You can also contact our office and we'll help you understand your specific requirements.

Please bring your insurance card, photo ID, a list of current medications, any relevant imaging studies (MRI, CT scans, X-rays) on disc if available, and medical records from previous treatments. A list of questions you'd like to discuss is also helpful.

We accept most major insurance plans. Please contact our office to verify that your specific plan is accepted. Our staff can help you understand your coverage and any out-of-pocket costs before your appointment.

Surgery is typically considered when conservative treatments haven't provided relief, when there's progressive neurological weakness, or when the condition poses significant health risks. Dr. Rubin will discuss all options with you and recommend surgery only when it's likely to provide meaningful benefit.

Recovery varies depending on the procedure and individual factors. Minimally invasive procedures often allow for faster recovery with less pain. Dr. Rubin will provide detailed recovery instructions and schedule follow-up appointments to monitor your progress. Many patients return to normal activities within weeks, though some procedures require longer recovery periods.

Seek immediate medical attention (call 911 or go to the emergency room) for: sudden severe headache, loss of consciousness, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, difficulty speaking or understanding speech, sudden vision changes, loss of bladder or bowel control, or trauma to the head or spine. These symptoms may indicate a medical emergency.
