About Dr. Rubin

A Colorado native who came home to practice.

Dr. Benjamin Rubin was born and raised in Denver. His interest in neuroscience began at the University of Colorado Boulder, where he majored in psychology and earned a certificate in behavioral neuroscience. He went on to medical school in Chicago and completed seven years of neurological surgery training in New York City, serving as chief resident at NYU Langone Health before returning to Colorado in 2015.

He has since cared for patients across the Front Range — first at the Colorado Brain and Spine Institute at Swedish Medical Center, then for seven years with Kaiser Permanente in Denver, and today within the Denver Metro area. He lives in Colorado with his wife and two young children, and spends his free time on the golf course.

Dr. Rubin aims for an open, honest, and empathetic conversation with every patient, so that people feel heard and leave with their questions actually answered. He has published research in journals including the Journal of Neurosurgery and Brain Injury.

“The most rewarding part of being a neurosurgeon is seeing patients achieve great outcomes by placing their trust in me and working together toward a common goal. Surgery is the vehicle we use to get there.”

Surgical Approach

The right technique for the goal of the operation.

Dr. Rubin uses both open and minimally invasive techniques, choosing whichever will best achieve the goals of surgery rather than defaulting to one approach.

Minimally invasive and open surgery

Some conditions are best treated through smaller exposures; others require a traditional open approach to be done well. The decision is made case by case, based on your anatomy, diagnosis, and what the operation needs to accomplish.

Frameless stereotactic navigation

Image-guided navigation maps your own imaging onto the operative field in real time, which is particularly valuable in cranial and complex spinal procedures.

A comprehensive treatment team

Dr. Rubin works alongside physiatry and interventional pain management physicians, physician assistants, and nurses, so your plan of care holds together from the first visit through recovery — and surgery is recommended only when it is genuinely the right answer.

New Patients

How to become a patient.

Knowing what to expect makes the first visit easier. Here is the usual path from referral to follow-up.

  1. Get a referral

    Most patients need a referral from their primary care physician or another specialist before scheduling. Our office can help you confirm what your plan requires.

  2. Complete your imaging

    A minimum set of imaging — typically an MRI or CT of the affected area — needs to be completed before your appointment so it can be reviewed with you in person.

  3. Consultation and plan

    Dr. Rubin reviews your history, examination, and images, explains what he sees, and discusses the options — including non-surgical care when that is the better course.

  4. Surgery and recovery

    If an operation is the right choice, you will know what it involves and what recovery looks like beforehand, with scheduled follow-up and a care team available by phone and secure message.
